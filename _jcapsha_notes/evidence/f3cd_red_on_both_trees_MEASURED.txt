WHY THIS IS A DIFFERENT STATUS FROM F1 AND F2, AND THE DIFFERENCE DECIDES THE
LANDING ORDER. F1 and F2 are red on `main` and GREEN on the fix branch: they are
correct guards waiting on that branch to land, and landing it turns them green.
F3c and F3d are red on BOTH. Landing the fix branch does not turn them green,
because they are not guarding a defect that branch repairs.

AND THE `(NO CALL AT ALL)` IS THE FINDING, not a detail of it. The fix branch
was built on the conclusion that `PAD_ROTATION_VERTICAL` is inert, so it
REMOVED the variable's effect rather than routing it to the sides the tool
documents it for. The tool does read it -- it drives north and south. So the
branch takes a variable that was misrouted and makes it genuinely inert, which
is the one outcome that makes the original diagnosis true after the fact.

NOT A REASON TO HOLD THE BRANCH. The site fix it exists for is real, verified on
four PDK trees, and independent of all of this. These two records are the
argument for a FOLLOW-UP, and they are filed as Bucket A with their reds shown
rather than attached to that branch as a review objection.
