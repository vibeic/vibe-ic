THE OPPOSITE SIDE IS A MIRROR IN THE TOOL AND A HALF TURN IN OURS.
Visible at the DEFAULT -- no declaration required.

MEASURED, both halves, at PAD_ROTATION_* all R0:

  ours  (drive pad_ring_gen, read the orientation it RECORDS per side)
        SOUTH = N     NORTH = S
        DEF spelling; the module's own alias table reads R0 -> N, R180 -> S.
        So ours derives NORTH as a 180-degree ROTATION of SOUTH.

  tool  (separate process, evidence/rotation_two_arm_MEASURED.txt)
        SOUTH = R0    NORTH = MX
        MX is DEF `FS`. So the tool derives NORTH as a MIRROR of SOUTH.

  N (R180) != FS (MX). A half turn and a mirror are different orientations.

THE TOOL SAYS SO IN ITS OWN SOURCE, and it is not ambiguous:

    const odb::dbOrientType south_rotation_ver = odb::dbOrientType::R0;
    const odb::dbOrientType north_rotation_ver = south_rotation_ver.flipX();
    odb::dbOrientType west_rotation_hor = odb::dbOrientType::MXR90;
    const odb::dbOrientType east_rotation_hor = west_rotation_hor.flipY();

  north = south.flipX()      east = west.flipY()      -- MIRRORS, both axes.

OURS:
    "N": PR.rotate_cw(cfg["rotation"]["PAD_ROTATION_HORIZONTAL"], 2)
    "E": PR.rotate_cw(cfg["rotation"]["PAD_ROTATION_VERTICAL"], 2)
  a half turn, both axes, and the helper's own docstring says so:
  "One quarter turn CLOCKWISE."

WHY IT MATTERS AND WHY NOTHING CAUGHT IT. For a rectangular footprint a half
turn and a mirror occupy the SAME bounding box, so every geometric check in this
step -- the fit sum, the spacing arithmetic, abutment, BTerm coverage -- agrees
under both. What differs is where the cell's PINS end up: a mirrored pad and a
rotated pad present their bond pad and their core-side signal on opposite edges.
A ring built this way passes every check the step makes and hands the DEF reader
a pad ring whose cells face the wrong way.

SEVERITY RELATIVE TO THE REST OF THIS CAPTURE. Every other finding here needs
somebody to DECLARE something before it becomes visible. This one is present at
the default, with no pad configuration written at all, on both axes.

NOT A NEW CLASS. It is the same one the other findings are: a re-implementation
that drifted from the upstream whose behaviour it claims to reproduce -- on the
input set, on the arithmetic, on the convention, and now on the transform.
