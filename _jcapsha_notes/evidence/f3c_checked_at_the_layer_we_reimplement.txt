F3c CHECKED AT THE LAYER WE ACTUALLY RE-IMPLEMENT -- and it holds.

I had compared our mapping against the TOOL's documentation. But we do not
re-implement the tool; we re-implement the FLOW layer above it, and that layer
is the one that names `PAD_ROTATION_HORIZONTAL`. If the flow layer documented it
our way, the attribution would move off us. So it had to be read.

WHAT THE FLOW LAYER SAYS, verbatim from its variable declarations:

    PAD_ROTATION_HORIZONTAL
        "Rotation to apply to the horizontal sites to ensure pads are placed
         correctly."     default R0, pdk=True

    PAD_ROTATION_VERTICAL
        "Rotation to apply to the vertical sites to ensure pads are placed
         correctly."     default R0, pdk=True

It says "horizontal SITES" -- the same phrase the tool uses -- and it adds no
redefinition of its own. It then passes the value straight through as
`-rotation_horizontal`, with the SAME site given for both `-horizontal_site` and
`-vertical_site`. So the flow layer inherits the tool's meaning unchanged, and
the tool's own page expands "horizontal site" as "the site for the horizontal
pads (EAST AND WEST)".

CONCLUSION: the flow layer is consistent with the tool. Neither is inverted.
Ours is. F3c's attribution stands, now checked at the layer it is about.

AND THIS IS ALSO HOW THE INVERSION HAPPENED, which is the generalisable part.
The layer we read documents the term TERSELY -- "the horizontal sites" -- and
never says which sides those are. The disambiguation exists, but it lives ONE
LAYER DOWN, in the tool's own command reference. A reader working from the flow
layer alone has a description that is complete-looking, authoritative, and
insufficient, and the natural reading of "horizontal" is the horizontal ROWS.
That reading is wrong and nothing at that layer says so.

THE GENERAL LESSON, and it is not about pads:

    WHEN THE LAYER YOU RE-IMPLEMENT DOCUMENTS A TERM TERSELY, THE DEFINITION
    MAY LIVE IN THE LAYER BELOW IT. A description that does not enumerate its
    cases is not a definition, however authoritative its source.

It is the same failure I made myself, one level up, when I filed a defect
against the tool after reading its argument NAMES and not its argument
DEFINITIONS. Twice in one capture, at two different layers, from the same
substitution: taking a name for a specification.
