DDR5 SDRAM
JEDEC Standard JESD79-5
Double Data Rate 5 Synchronous Dynamic Random Access Memory

JEDEC Solid State Technology Association


=====================================================================
1. OVERVIEW
=====================================================================

DDR5 SDRAM (Double Data Rate 5 Synchronous Dynamic Random Access
Memory) is the fifth-generation mainstream double-data-rate DRAM
defined by the JEDEC Standard JESD79-5. DDR5 is the successor to
DDR4 (JESD79-4) and targets higher bandwidth, higher capacity, and
improved channel efficiency at lower operating voltage. DDR5 raises
the per-pin data rate from the DDR4 ceiling of 3200 MT/s up to
DDR5-8400 and beyond, while reducing VDD/VDDQ from 1.2 V (DDR4) to
1.1 V (DDR5).

The single most defining architectural change in DDR5 relative to
DDR4 is the split of each DIMM channel into TWO INDEPENDENT
SUB-CHANNELS. A DDR4 DIMM presents a single 64-bit data bus (72-bit
with ECC). A DDR5 DIMM instead presents two independent 32-bit
sub-channels (40-bit each with ECC), each with its own command/address
(CA) bus and its own chip-select. The two sub-channels operate
independently, which improves concurrency and effective bandwidth for
the burst length of 16 (BL16) that DDR5 uses.

Key DDR5 generational features grounded in JESD79-5:
  - Two independent 32-bit (40-bit with ECC) sub-channels per DIMM.
  - Burst Length 16 (BL16) as the native burst, plus BL32 burst-chop.
  - Decision Feedback Equalization (DFE) on the DQ receivers.
  - On-die ECC (ODECC) inside every DDR5 SDRAM device.
  - 32 banks organized in 8 bank groups (x8 device); same-bank refresh.
  - VDD = VDDQ = 1.1 V; VPP = 1.8 V (wordline boost), as in DDR4.
  - DIMM-level Power Management IC (PMIC) on the module (12 V -> 1.1 V).
  - On-module SPD hub and, for RDIMMs, a Registering Clock Driver (RCD).
  - Internal VrefDQ and VrefCA generation with read/write/CA training.
  - A new 2-cycle CA command protocol (different from DDR4's 1-cycle).
  - Greatly expanded Mode Register space (MR0..MRxx, many more than DDR4).
  - Duty-Cycle Adjuster (DCA) and Decision-Feedback per-DQ equalization.

DDR5 is a parallel, source-synchronous, double-data-rate memory:
data is captured on both edges of a forwarded differential data strobe
(DQS_t/DQS_c), and commands/addresses are captured relative to the
differential clock (CK_t/CK_c). DDR5 is NOT a serial/embedded-clock
link and NOT a packetized interconnect; it is a JEDEC parallel DRAM.

DDR5 device organizations: x4, x8, and x16. The x8 device has 32 banks
in 8 bank groups (4 banks per bank group); the x16 device has 16 banks
in 4 bank groups (the wider DQ trades bank count for data width).


=====================================================================
2. TWO INDEPENDENT SUB-CHANNEL ARCHITECTURE
=====================================================================

A DDR5 UDIMM/RDIMM is partitioned into two independent sub-channels,
commonly labeled Sub-Channel A and Sub-Channel B.

  - Each sub-channel is 32 data bits wide (DQ[31:0]) plus, when ECC is
    present, 8 additional bits, giving 40 bits per sub-channel.
  - Each sub-channel has its OWN command/address (CA) bus, its OWN
    chip-select(s) (CS_n), and its OWN clock (CK_t/CK_c).
  - The two sub-channels are addressed and trained independently. A
    read or write on Sub-Channel A is unrelated to traffic on
    Sub-Channel B.

This contrasts with DDR4, where a DIMM presents a single 64-bit
(72-bit ECC) data path shared by one CA bus. The DDR5 two-sub-channel
split, combined with BL16, keeps each access at a 64-byte cache-line
granularity (32 bits x 16 = 64 bytes) per sub-channel, doubling the
number of independent memory accesses a DIMM can service.

Sub-channel benefits:
  - Higher effective bandwidth via concurrency.
  - Finer access granularity matched to 64-byte cache lines at BL16.
  - Independent training and calibration per sub-channel.


=====================================================================
3. DECISION FEEDBACK EQUALIZATION (DFE)
=====================================================================

DDR5 introduces Decision Feedback Equalization (DFE) on the DQ
receivers inside the SDRAM. As per-pin data rates climb past
4800 MT/s, inter-symbol interference (ISI) from the channel degrades
the data eye. DFE is a non-linear equalizer that uses previously
decided bits to cancel post-cursor ISI on the current bit, reopening
the eye without amplifying noise the way a linear equalizer would.

  - DFE is applied on the DQ receiver path of the DDR5 SDRAM.
  - The DFE tap coefficients are programmed through Mode Registers and
    are tuned during write training.
  - DDR5 also defines a Duty-Cycle Adjuster (DCA) and per-DQ Vref
    (VrefDQ) trimming to center the sampling point.

DFE is a DDR5-specific receiver feature; DDR3 and DDR4 SDRAM do not
employ decision feedback equalization on the DQ receiver.


=====================================================================
4. ON-DIE ECC (ODECC)
=====================================================================

Every DDR5 SDRAM device implements On-Die ECC (ODECC) as a standard
feature. On-die ECC is a single-error-correcting code computed and
checked entirely inside the DRAM die, transparent to the host.

  - On a write, the DDR5 device computes ECC check bits over the
    written data and stores them in spare cells alongside the data.
  - On a read, the device checks/corrects single-bit errors before
    the data leaves the die.
  - On-die ECC corrects single-bit failures that arise from the small
    cell capacitance and aggressive process scaling of DDR5, improving
    yield and reliability.
  - On-die ECC is INDEPENDENT of the optional system/link ECC carried
    on the extra 8 bits of each 40-bit sub-channel; the two ECC layers
    are complementary.

DDR5 also defines Error Check and Scrub (ECS), a mode where the device
internally reads, corrects, and writes back data to scrub accumulated
single-bit errors, and can report error counts through Mode Registers.


=====================================================================
5. BANKS, BANK GROUPS, AND SAME-BANK REFRESH
=====================================================================

DDR5 organizes its array into banks grouped into bank groups:
  - x4 / x8 device: 32 banks in 8 bank groups (4 banks per group).
  - x16 device: 16 banks in 4 bank groups (4 banks per group).

Bank groups allow back-to-back accesses to different groups to hide
the column-to-column delay (tCCD_L within a group vs tCCD_S across
groups), as in DDR4 but with twice the bank count.

Refresh:
  - DDR5 retains all-bank refresh (REFab) and adds SAME-BANK REFRESH
    (REFsb), where a refresh targets the same bank number across all
    bank groups, leaving the other banks available for access.
  - Same-bank refresh improves availability by allowing the controller
    to keep other banks open during a refresh.
  - DDR5 supports fine-granularity refresh and an extended temperature
    refresh range (auto self-refresh / temperature-controlled refresh).


=====================================================================
6. BURST LENGTH (BL16 / BL32 BURST-CHOP)
=====================================================================

DDR5 native burst length is BL16: a single column access transfers 16
beats of data on DQ (16 x 32 bits per sub-channel = 64 bytes), matched
to a 64-byte cache line. DDR5 also supports BL32 (burst-chop) and the
on-the-fly burst-length control via the CA command.

The doubling of native burst length from BL8 (DDR4) to BL16 (DDR5),
combined with the narrower 32-bit sub-channel, preserves the 64-byte
access granularity while doubling the number of independent accesses.


=====================================================================
7. SPEED BINS AND DATA RATES
=====================================================================

DDR5 speed bins span from DDR5-3200 up to DDR5-8400 and beyond. Each
bin names the data rate in MT/s (mega-transfers per second):

  DDR5-3200   3200 MT/s   1600 MHz clock
  DDR5-3600   3600 MT/s   1800 MHz clock
  DDR5-4000   4000 MT/s   2000 MHz clock
  DDR5-4400   4400 MT/s   2200 MHz clock
  DDR5-4800   4800 MT/s   2400 MHz clock
  DDR5-5200   5200 MT/s   2600 MHz clock
  DDR5-5600   5600 MT/s   2800 MHz clock
  DDR5-6000   6000 MT/s   3000 MHz clock
  DDR5-6400   6400 MT/s   3200 MHz clock
  DDR5-7200   7200 MT/s   3600 MHz clock
  DDR5-8000   8000 MT/s   4000 MHz clock
  DDR5-8400   8400 MT/s   4200 MHz clock

Data is double-data-rate: two beats per clock period (one per CK edge),
so DDR5-6400 has a 3200 MHz clock and 6400 MT/s per DQ pin.


=====================================================================
8. SUPPLY VOLTAGES
=====================================================================

DDR5 lowers the core/IO supply relative to DDR4:
  - VDD  = 1.1 V (core)        (DDR4: 1.2 V)
  - VDDQ = 1.1 V (DQ IO)       (DDR4: 1.2 V)
  - VPP  = 1.8 V (wordline pump, same as DDR4)

The lower 1.1 V VDD/VDDQ reduces dynamic and IO power at iso-frequency.
On a DIMM, these rails are produced on-module by the PMIC from the
12 V host supply (see Section 11).


=====================================================================
9. INTERNAL VREF AND TRAINING (VrefDQ / VrefCA)
=====================================================================

DDR5 moves the reference voltages on-die and adds extensive training:
  - VrefDQ is generated and trimmed INSIDE each DDR5 device (per-DQ in
    some implementations) instead of being supplied externally as in
    earlier generations. VrefDQ is tuned during read/write training.
  - VrefCA is the internal reference for the command/address inputs and
    is tuned during CA training.

Training sequences defined by DDR5:
  - CA training (command/address bus deskew and VrefCA centering).
  - Write Leveling (align DQS to CK at the DRAM).
  - Read training / read DQ calibration (per-DQ deskew, VrefDQ center).
  - Write training (DFE tap tuning, write VrefDQ center, write deskew).
  - MPC (Multi-Purpose Command) drives many training and feature modes.

Training is performed independently per sub-channel.


=====================================================================
10. CA (COMMAND/ADDRESS) PROTOCOL
=====================================================================

DDR5 uses a NEW command/address protocol that differs from DDR4:
  - The CA bus is CA[13:0] with a chip-select CS_n per sub-channel.
  - Commands are TWO CA-CYCLE commands (a command occupies two
    consecutive UI on the CA bus), unlike DDR4's single-cycle command
    encoding. This 2-cycle CA protocol allows a wider command space and
    carries more address bits per command.
  - Core DRAM commands: ACTIVATE (ACT), READ (RD), WRITE (WR),
    PRECHARGE (PRE), REFRESH (REF, REFab/REFsb), SELF REFRESH ENTRY/EXIT,
    Mode Register Write (MRW) / Mode Register Read (MRR), Multi-Purpose
    Command (MPC), and Precharge-All.
  - Timing parameters: tRCD (RAS-to-CAS delay), tRP (row precharge),
    tRAS (row active time), tRFC (refresh cycle time), tCCD_L / tCCD_S
    (column-to-column, same/different bank group), tWR (write recovery),
    CL (CAS latency).

The activate-precharge row cycle is the same conceptual DRAM access
sequence used by prior DDR generations (ACTIVATE a row, READ/WRITE
columns, PRECHARGE the row), but encoded in the DDR5 2-cycle CA format.


=====================================================================
11. DIMM-LEVEL POWER (PMIC), SPD HUB, AND RCD
=====================================================================

DDR5 moves power management from the motherboard onto the DIMM:
  - DIMM PMIC (Power Management IC): a 12 V-input regulator on the
    module that generates the 1.1 V VDD/VDDQ and 1.8 V VPP rails locally
    for the DRAMs on that DIMM. Moving the PMIC on-module improves rail
    integrity and per-DIMM power control (a DDR4 DIMM had no on-module
    PMIC; rails came from the motherboard).
  - SPD Hub: the DDR5 Serial Presence Detect device is a hub that holds
    the module configuration EEPROM and buffers the sideband I2C/I3C
    bus to the on-module devices (PMIC, RCD, temperature sensors).
  - RCD (Registering Clock Driver): on a Registered DIMM (RDIMM) the RCD
    re-drives and registers the CK and CA signals to all DRAMs to
    improve signal integrity at high data rates. (UDIMMs have no RCD;
    LRDIMMs add data buffers (DB) as well.)

The on-module PMIC + SPD hub + RCD set is a DDR5-specific module
architecture; DDR3/DDR4 DIMMs did not carry an on-module PMIC.


=====================================================================
12. MODE REGISTERS
=====================================================================

DDR5 greatly expands the Mode Register (MR) space relative to DDR4.
Where DDR4 had a handful of mode registers (MR0..MR7), DDR5 defines
many more (MR0 through MRxx, dozens of registers) covering, among
others:
  - MR0: Burst length, CAS latency (read latency).
  - MR2: write latency, 2N mode.
  - Mode registers for DFE tap coefficients.
  - Mode registers for VrefDQ / VrefCA settings.
  - Mode registers for on-die ECC control and error reporting.
  - Mode registers for refresh management (RFM), ECS scrubbing.
  - Mode registers for duty-cycle adjustment (DCA) and Tx/Rx
    equalization.
Mode Register Write (MRW) and Mode Register Read (MRR) commands access
these registers over the 2-cycle CA protocol.


=====================================================================
13. PIN / SIGNAL LIST (per sub-channel)
=====================================================================

  CK_t / CK_c     Differential clock (command/address timing reference)
  CS_n            Chip select (per sub-channel)
  CA[13:0]        Command/address bus (2-cycle commands)
  DQ[31:0]        Data bus (32 bits per sub-channel; 40 with ECC)
  DQS_t / DQS_c   Differential data strobe (source-synchronous, per byte)
  DM_n / DBI_n    Data mask / data bus inversion (per byte lane)
  CKE             Clock enable / power-down control
  RESET_n         Asynchronous reset
  ALERT_n         CRC / command-address parity error alert
  ZQ              External calibration reference resistor
  VDD / VDDQ      1.1 V supplies
  VPP             1.8 V wordline-boost supply
  Vref            Internal (VrefDQ / VrefCA generated on-die)

DDR5 supports Command/Address parity (CA parity) and write CRC, with
errors reported on ALERT_n.


=====================================================================
14. DISTINCTION FROM SIBLING MEMORY STANDARDS
=====================================================================

DDR5 (JESD79-5) is distinguished from related JEDEC memory standards:

  - vs DDR3 (JESD79-3): DDR3 is 1.5 V, uses a DLL, a single 64/72-bit
    data bus, BL8, no bank groups, no DFE, no sub-channels, and no
    on-module PMIC. DDR5 is 1.1 V, two 32-bit sub-channels, BL16, DFE,
    on-die ECC, DIMM PMIC.
  - vs DDR4 (JESD79-4): DDR4 is 1.2 V, has bank groups and a single
    64/72-bit bus, BL8, gear-down mode, NO DFE, NO sub-channels, NO
    on-die ECC standard, and NO on-module PMIC. DDR5's defining
    additions over DDR4 are the two sub-channels, DFE, on-die ECC,
    same-bank refresh, and the DIMM PMIC/SPD-hub.
  - vs LPDDR5 (JESD209-5): LPDDR5 is the low-power mobile generation
    using a separate Write Clock (WCK) running at a multiple of CK, a
    low-power architecture, deep self-refresh and Dynamic Voltage/
    Frequency Scaling. DDR5 has NO WCK; it is the mainstream
    (non-low-power) generation timed off CK with DQS strobes.
  - vs HBM3 (JESD238): HBM3 is a 3D-stacked DRAM with through-silicon
    vias (TSV), a very wide 1024-bit interface, and pseudo-channels.
    DDR5 is a planar (non-stacked) DRAM with no TSV and a 32-bit
    sub-channel data width.

The DDR5-defining structural signature is the combination of:
JESD79-5 + two independent 32-bit (40-bit ECC) sub-channels per DIMM
+ Decision Feedback Equalization (DFE) on DQ + on-die ECC + same-bank
refresh + DIMM-level PMIC / SPD-hub, at VDD/VDDQ = 1.1 V with BL16.
