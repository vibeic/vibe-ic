IEEE 802.3 Management Data Input/Output (MDIO) Interface
Clause 22 and Clause 45 Management Interface Specification
IEEE Std 802.3, Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers

1. Introduction
The Management Data Input/Output (MDIO) interface, defined in IEEE 802.3 Clause 22
and extended in Clause 45, is a simple, low-speed, two-wire serial management bus
between a Station Management entity (STA) and one or more PHY devices. The STA is
typically embedded in the Media Access Control (MAC) layer device; the managed
entity is a PHY (Clause 22) or an MDIO Manageable Device (MMD) (Clause 45). MDIO
gives the STA read and write access to the management registers inside the PHY/MMD,
allowing it to read link status, configure auto-negotiation, and control PHY
operating modes. MDIO is also referred to as the MII Management interface (MIIM).

2. Signal Definition
The MDIO interface uses exactly two signals:
  MDC  - Management Data Clock. A clock sourced (driven) by the STA. It is an
         aperiodic clock with no minimum frequency; the maximum frequency is
         2.5 MHz (minimum period 400 ns). The PHY/MMD samples MDIO on the rising
         edge of MDC.
  MDIO - Management Data Input/Output. A bidirectional data line. It is an
         open-drain (or open-collector) signal that requires an external pull-up
         resistor (typically 1.5 kohm to 10 kohm) to a supply (1.2 V to 3.3 V).
         The STA drives MDIO during the command portion of a frame; the PHY drives
         MDIO during the data portion of a read. Multiple PHYs share the same MDC
         and MDIO; the PHYAD field selects which one responds.

MDIO has NO start condition, NO stop condition, and NO per-byte acknowledge. It is
NOT I2C: there is no SDA/SCL start/stop signalling and no 7-bit device addressing
with ACK. Instead every transaction is a fixed-length frame preceded by a preamble.

3. Clause 22 Frame Format
A Clause 22 management frame is 64 bit times long: a 32-bit preamble followed by a
32-bit management frame. The 32 bits after the preamble are organized as:
  PRE   - Preamble: 32 contiguous logic-one bits driven by the STA on MDIO to
          synchronize the PHY. The PHY may operate in preamble-suppression mode.
  ST    - Start of Frame: 2 bits. For Clause 22 the Start pattern is 01.
  OP    - Operation Code: 2 bits. 10 = Read, 01 = Write.
  PHYAD - PHY Address: 5 bits, MSB first. Selects one of up to 32 PHYs (0..31).
  REGAD - Register Address: 5 bits, MSB first. Selects one of 32 registers (0..31).
  TA    - Turnaround: 2 bits. A bus-direction reversal time. For a write the STA
          drives 10. For a read the STA tri-states (Z) during the first TA bit and
          the PHY drives 0 during the second TA bit, i.e. Z0.
  DATA  - Data: 16 bits, MSB first. For a write the STA drives the data; for a read
          the PHY drives the data.
The total management frame after preamble is ST(2) + OP(2) + PHYAD(5) + REGAD(5) +
TA(2) + DATA(16) = 32 bits.

4. Clause 22 Register Set (the basic register set, REGAD 0..15)
  Reg 0   Control Register (BMCR)   - Reset (bit 15), Loopback (bit 14), Speed
                                       Selection, Auto-Negotiation Enable (bit 12),
                                       Power Down (bit 11), Isolate, Restart
                                       Auto-Negotiation (bit 9), Duplex Mode (bit 8).
  Reg 1   Status Register (BMSR)    - Link Status (bit 2), Auto-Negotiation Complete
                                       (bit 5), Remote Fault, capability bits.
  Reg 2   PHY Identifier 1          - Bits 3:18 of the Organizationally Unique ID.
  Reg 3   PHY Identifier 2          - OUI bits, model number, revision number.
  Reg 4   Auto-Negotiation Advertisement.
  Reg 5   Auto-Negotiation Link Partner Ability.
  Reg 6   Auto-Negotiation Expansion.

5. Clause 45 Frame Format
Clause 45 extends MDIO to manage devices with more than 32 registers (e.g. 10G+
PHYs) using indirect addressing across multiple MMDs. The Clause 45 frame uses the
same field layout but changes the encodings:
  PRE   - Preamble: 32 ones (suppressible).
  ST    - Start of Frame: 2 bits. For Clause 45 the Start pattern is 00 (this is
          how a PHY tells Clause 45 from Clause 22, whose ST is 01).
  OP    - Operation Code: 2 bits.
            00 = Address     (load the 16-bit register address into the MMD)
            01 = Write       (write the addressed register)
            11 = Read        (read the addressed register)
            10 = Read-and-Increment-Address (read, then post-increment the address)
  PRTAD - Port Address: 5 bits. Selects one of up to 32 ports (replaces PHYAD).
  DEVAD - Device Address: 5 bits. Selects the MMD inside the port.
  TA    - Turnaround: 2 bits (10 for address/write, Z0 for read).
  DATA  - 16 bits. In an Address frame this is the register address to load; in a
          read/write frame this is the register data.
Indirect addressing: the STA first issues an Address frame (OP=00) to load the
16-bit register address into the addressed MMD's address register, then issues
Write (OP=01), Read (OP=11) or Read-and-Increment (OP=10) frames that operate on
the previously loaded address.

6. Clause 45 MMD Device Addresses (DEVAD)
  1   PMA/PMD          (Physical Medium Attachment / Physical Medium Dependent)
  2   WIS              (WAN Interface Sublayer)
  3   PCS              (Physical Coding Sublayer)
  4   PHY XS           (PHY Extender Sublayer)
  5   DTE XS           (DTE Extender Sublayer)
  6   TC               (Transmission Convergence)
  7   Auto-Negotiation
  29  Clause 22 extension
  30  Vendor-specific device 1
  31  Vendor-specific device 2

7. Turnaround Operation
The Turnaround (TA) field provides bus-direction reversal between the STA-driven
command portion and the PHY-driven data portion. On a read the STA releases MDIO to
high-impedance (Z) during the first TA bit time; the PHY then drives MDIO to 0
during the second TA bit time before clocking out the 16-bit data MSB first. On a
write there is no direction change: the STA drives TA = 10 and continues to drive
the 16 data bits. This avoids both the STA and the PHY driving MDIO simultaneously.

8. Electrical and Timing Characteristics
  MDC maximum frequency        2.5 MHz
  MDC minimum period           400 ns
  MDIO setup to MDC rising     10 ns (min), STA-sourced data
  MDIO hold from MDC rising    10 ns (min)
  MDIO output delay from MDC   0 ns to 300 ns (PHY-sourced data, Clause 22)
  MDIO drive                   open-drain, external pull-up 1.5 k to 10 k
  Preamble length              32 MDC cycles (one bit per MDC rising edge)
  Supply for pull-up           1.2 V to 3.3 V

9. Addressing and Multi-PHY Operation
Up to 32 PHYs (Clause 22) or 32 ports (Clause 45) share one MDC/MDIO pair. The
5-bit PHYAD/PRTAD selects which PHY responds; non-selected PHYs leave MDIO
high-impedance. The STA must use a unique PHYAD per PHY, configured by hardware
strap pins on each PHY. Address 0 is sometimes reserved as a broadcast/isolate
address depending on the PHY vendor.

10. Differences from I2C, SPI and JTAG (non-normative)
  - Versus I2C: MDIO has no START/STOP conditions, no SDA/SCL bus-arbitration, no
    per-byte ACK, and a fixed 32-bit frame instead of arbitrary byte streams. PHY
    selection is by a 5-bit PHYAD field inside the frame, not a 7-bit slave address
    with an ACK bit.
  - Versus SPI: MDIO is a 2-wire half-duplex bus with a turnaround, not a 4-wire
    full-duplex SCK/MOSI/MISO/CS shift link; there is no separate chip-select wire.
  - Versus JTAG: MDIO is a register-access management bus, not a 4/5-wire boundary
    scan TAP (TCK/TMS/TDI/TDO/TRST) with an instruction register and a 16-state
    TAP controller state machine.

11. Reset and Initialization
There is no dedicated reset wire on MDIO. A PHY is reset via the Control Register
(Clause 22 Reg 0 bit 15 = 1, self-clearing) or via the MMD control register
(Clause 45). On power-up the STA typically reads the Status Register (Reg 1) and
the PHY Identifier registers (Reg 2 / Reg 3) to discover the attached PHY before
configuring auto-negotiation.
